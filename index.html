Professional codes of ethics

A code of ethics is a formal set of principles and guidelines that govern professional behavior, ensuring integrity and accountability in practice. It gives IT professionals a moral compass, builds trust with clients and society, prevents misuse of information systems, and strengthens credibility.

Core tenets for Filipino IT professionals

Public welfare

Consider the general welfare and public good in the performance of your work.

Public knowledge

Actively promote public knowledge, understanding, and appreciation of Information Technology.

Truthful representation

Advertise goods and services clearly and truthfully, accurately stating your areas of competence and capabilities.

Intellectual property

Strictly comply with intellectual property and patent laws, with no unauthorized use of proprietary software or output.

Professionalism

Accept full responsibility for undertaken work and use your skills with maximum competence.

Confidentiality

Do not disclose confidential information obtained during professional duties without consent, unless legally required.

Quality assurance

Strive for the highest quality in both the products and services you offer.

Unlawful acts

Do not knowingly take part in developing IT systems that promote fraud or other unlawful acts.

Continuing education

Uphold and improve IT standards through continuing professional development and memberships in relevant associations.

Key principles

Integrity

Act with honesty and fairness; avoid deception and fraud.

Example: report vulnerabilities truthfully instead of hiding them.

Confidentiality

Protect sensitive information and respect client privacy.

Example: never share student or patient records without consent.

Professional competence

Keep skills current and deliver work that meets industry standards.

Example: attend cybersecurity training.

Respect for intellectual property

Acknowledge ownership; avoid plagiarism and piracy.

Example: use licensed software, not pirated copies.

Compare the codes

The Philippine Computer Society code sets standards for IT and IS practitioners in the Philippines. It commits members to honesty and fairness, data confidentiality, avoiding conflicts of interest, and continuous development. It is localized, for example around Data Privacy Act compliance.

The Association for Computing Machinery code emphasizes contributing to society and human well-being, with a strong focus on avoiding harm and respecting privacy.

The Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers code highlights honesty, fairness, and avoiding conflicts of interest, and stresses responsibility for public safety and welfare.

All three codes converge on integrity, competence, and respect for rights. ACM and IEEE give a global view; PCS fits the Philippine legal context.

Responsibilities of IS professionals

Protect data privacy and security

Safeguard sensitive information from unauthorized access, breaches, and misuse. Use encryption, strong authentication and access controls, and regular audits and vulnerability testing. A clinic storing patient records must keep them confidential even when accessed remotely.

Promote accessibility and inclusivity

Design systems usable by everyone, including persons with disabilities. Follow standards such as WCAG, design for diverse languages and cultures, and offer screen readers and captions. A university portal should work for visually impaired students.

Maintain professional competence

Technology changes quickly, so attend workshops and certifications, join professional communities, and follow AI, cybersecurity, and cloud trends. Outdated or incompetent work can harm clients and organizations.

Protect organizational and client interests

Act in the best interest of employers and clients while balancing public welfare: avoid conflicts of interest, protect intellectual property, and communicate risks openly. A software engineer should disclose flaws even if it delays delivery.

Act with social responsibility

Consider technology's wider impact: avoid harmful uses such as surveillance misuse, build energy-efficient systems, and support digital literacy. The debate over AI in criminal sentencing shows the tension between efficiency and fairness.

Accountability and professional conduct

What accountability means

Accountability is the obligation to take responsibility for your actions, decisions, and the outcomes of the systems you design or manage. That means owning successes and failures, keeping processes transparent, and accepting the consequences of misconduct or negligence.

A system administrator must acknowledge responsibility if a breach happens because of weak security.

Professional conduct

  • Be honest with clients and colleagues
  • Respect confidentiality agreements
  • Avoid conflicts of interest
  • Stay professional in emails and social media

Philippine laws

  • Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173): protects personal information, creates the National Privacy Commission (NPC), requires a Data Protection Officer, and penalizes unauthorized disclosure or negligence.
  • Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012 (RA 10175)
  • Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines

Misconduct can lead to fines, imprisonment, or loss of license.

Lessons from failures

Local: data breaches in government agencies exposed citizen records. Global: the Facebook–Cambridge Analytica scandal involved misuse of personal data for political profiling.

  • Be transparent and get user consent
  • Comply strictly with privacy laws
  • Ethical duty goes beyond legal minimums

Transparency means clear communication about data use, regular reporting of risks, and open disclosure of errors, as when a bank tells customers about an outage right away.

Ethical decision-making and compliance

Four frameworks

Utilitarian

Choose the option that maximizes benefit and minimizes harm for the most people.

Rights-based

Respect privacy, freedom of expression, and access to information.

Justice and fairness

Promote equality and impartiality in systems and processes.

Professional codes

Use ACM and IEEE codes as guiding principles.

Example: an AI recruitment system must weigh efficiency against fairness so it does not discriminate against protected groups.

GDPR

Applies to organizations handling EU citizens' data. Its principles are lawfulness, fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity, and confidentiality. It requires explicit consent, data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure), and breach notification within 72 hours.

Philippine Data Privacy Act

Protects the personal information of Filipino citizens, sets up the NPC to enforce compliance, and requires a Data Protection Officer, security measures, and respect for data subject rights. In practice: encrypt sensitive data, publish clear privacy policies with consent, and run regular audits.

Balancing innovation with regulation

AI, IoT, and blockchain often outpace the law, and compliance can feel like a brake on developers. Three strategies help: build legal checks into the development lifecycle (proactive compliance), design privacy, fairness, and transparency as core features (ethical innovation), and work with regulators and industry groups (collaboration). A fintech startup, for instance, must follow anti-money laundering laws while keeping payments smooth.

Case studies

Open each case, discuss the questions with your group, then compare your reasoning with the codes above.

Data privacy breach in a government agency

A government database was hacked because of weak security. Addresses and ID numbers leaked online.

  • Who is accountable: IT staff, management, or the hackers?
  • Which professional conduct was violated?
  • How could transparency have helped rebuild public trust?
Concealing system flaws

A developer finds a critical flaw in a banking system, but management pressures them to hide it to meet a deadline.

  • What are the ethical and legal implications of concealing it?
  • How should the developer respond?
  • How does transparency protect both the company and its clients?
Misuse of client data

A consultant uses client data for personal gain, breaking confidentiality agreements.

  • Which laws and principles are violated?
  • What conduct should have been observed?
  • How can organizations prevent this?
